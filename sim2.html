Wait—Is There Even A Plot?

The Big Secret About This Book

Here is the #1 thing you need to know so you don't look clueless in ELA: There is no traditional fictional plot! It is a collection of 44 personal essays (non-fiction) where author John Green reviews random parts of human existence on a 5-star scale—just like Yelp reviews.

What does "Anthropocene" mean?

It's the current geological age where humans are the #1 force shaping planet Earth (climate, plastic, internet, tacos, viral pandemics). So John is literally reviewing what it feels like to be human right now.

♥ The Hidden "Story Arc" Across the Book

Even though each chapter is a standalone essay, your teacher will ask how the book evolves from beginning to end. Use these 3 stages:

PART 1: BEGINNING ★☆☆☆☆

Anxiety & Nostalgia

John starts out feeling overwhelmed by the modern world, his own OCD/anxiety, and how fast things change. He looks backward at childhood memories (like Diet Dr Pepper and Super Mario Kart) trying to find comfort.

Key Vibe: "Everything is weird and scary."
PART 2: MIDDLE ★★★☆☆

Isolation & The Pandemic

Written during the 2020 COVID-19 lockdowns, the essays get darker and lonelier. John explores humanity's destructive side (species extinction, plague, war) and admits he feels trapped in his own head.

Key Vibe: "Humans mess things up, and I'm stuck inside."
PART 3: ENDING ★★★★★

Radical Hope & Connection

By the final essays (like Auld Lang Syne and Three Farmers on Their Way to a Dance), John realizes that paying attention to ordinary beauty and loving other people is what saves us. Hope isn't naive—it's a choice.

Key Vibe: "We're flawed, and we're here because we're here."
